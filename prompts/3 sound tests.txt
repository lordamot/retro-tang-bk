Both answers come from the wiring table in the README and the sound section of platform.md.

1) Two USB cables at once. Your concern is fair, and I should have qualified what I said. The seven wires include a +5 wire, and on both boards that pin is the USB connector's VBUS with nothing in between. So with both cables in, the laptop's two ports are tied together through that wire.

- In practice this is low risk when both ports are on the same laptop. They sit within a few tens of millivolts of each other, the equalising current is small, and USB ports have overcurrent protection. This is how MiSTeryNano users flash the BL616 with it still wired up.
- It is a real risk when the two sources differ: one board on a phone charger and the other on the laptop, or a hub with its own supply. Then one supply back-feeds the other, and hosts are not designed to sink current on VBUS.
- The clean way: only one cable carries power at a time. For flashing the BL616, plug in the Dock's cable alone. The +5 wire then feeds the Tang Nano from the Dock, which is fine. For flashing the FPGA, plug in the Tang Nano alone, and the Dock is fed from it as in normal use. If you want both plugged in for a long session with the UART on the Tang Nano and the BL616 console on the Dock, pull the +5 wire while doing it.

Note the Tang Nano's SRAM bitstream is lost at power-off, so a bitstream loaded into SRAM goes away when you swap cables. Flash it to SPI flash for a session that swaps cables.

2) The AZBK's sound devices, as this design implements them after GID's emulator:

- The BK's own speaker, bits 6, 5, 2 of 177716, mutable by bit 2 of 177212.
- Two AY chips at 177172-177175, either YM2149 or AY8910 by bit 3 of 177212, plus the BK world's Spectrum-style access through 177714.
- The Covox: 16-bit stereo at 177200-177206, and the legacy 8-bit Covox on 177714.
- The sound DMA at 177160-177170: 16-bit PCM mono, IMA ADPCM mono and IMA ADPCM stereo, all at 44100 Hz, streamed from the 32 MB space.

The mix is MAXIOL's: right channel is A plus B of both AYs, left is C plus B, and everything else goes to both. The result leaves the board on the Dock's I2S DAC and inside the HDMI stream. Nothing else is in the register map: no MIDI, no General Sound, no SID. Status of all this on the board is in progress.md, and the open item there is noise on the sound.

hm. what does F11 do? it blanks the screen immediately and then OSD works but even cold reset does not help till tang replug

****

F11 is the СБР (reset) key. The firmware sends a press and a release flag, and keyboard.v keeps a level from them that refills the machine's 16 ms reset hold while it is up. The module handled key events only while the machine was not in reset; the press itself starts a reset and raises INIT, so the release arrived while events were ignored, the level never cleared, and the machine sat in reset. The OSD's cold reset pulses the same hold and cannot clear a level the keyboard owns; only a replug could. Fixed by taking the flagged keys (СТОП, СБР, the hotkeys) whether or not INIT is up. Lint clean; a before/after simulation with the new +RESETKEY_MS= option, the bitstream and a firmware rebuild for the 0.1.20 caption are running.

what is the difference between reset and cold reset in osd menu? they seem behave the same

****

On this design they are the same thing, by construction. Reset sends 'R' as 1 then 0: a level on the machine's reset request whose rising edge is the cold pulse. Cold reset (AZ) sends 'B', which sysctrl.v turns into a one-clock pulse into the same 16 ms hold, with the same cold pulse. Every reset here is a cold reset of the AZ controller (top.v's comment lists the OSD's 'R', S1, command 037 and the СБР key): the mapper's windows cleared, page 100 mapped, 177716 answering 170000 again, the page table emptied - both reboot through AZBOOT. The two entries exist because the siblings' menus have both. A distinct warm reset would be the real BK's reset button (the AZ keeps its mapper, the machine comes up in the BK monitor at 140000/100000, as GID's emulator does on a plain reset); not implemented, and adding it means deciding what the AZ keeps. Your call.

ok, next one while waiting. docs state that AZ controller itself supports switching standard BK display to be shown as from color output or as from bw one - do we support it?

****

Not yet. The AZ's legacy screen is a mode of 177230 (bits 2:0 = 0: 1 bit a pixel, 512 wide, the monochrome pair; 1: 2 bits, 256 wide, the 4-colour set 177662 picks); software sets it and the BK's own programs never touch it. The switch is the controller's hotkey АР2+ЛАТ (Alt+Win), and the palette reset АР2+РУС (Alt+Left Ctrl): the firmware decodes both and keyboard.v turns them into a one-clock pulse that goes nowhere in top.v. To support it the FPGA would toggle 177230 between the two forms on hotkey 1 and reload the legacy palette cells on hotkey 2; a small change, untested against what MAXIOL's controller does exactly. Your call.
